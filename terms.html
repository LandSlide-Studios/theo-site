3. Description of Service

Theo is a faith-based productivity application for iOS that combines personalized Bible study with voluntary app blocking functionality, social features, journaling capabilities, and AI-powered study assistance. The App is designed to help users build intentional screen habits by replacing passive device usage with meaningful Scripture engagement and community connection.

The App's features include, but are not limited to:

  • User Accounts. Sign in with Apple or Google to create an account, which syncs your profile, subscription, reading progress, streaks, journals, and social connections across your devices.

  • AI Bible Study Companion. An artificial intelligence-powered conversational assistant ("Theo") that provides Bible study support, devotional content, and faith-based encouragement. Theo is powered by third-party AI models operated by Anthropic and OpenAI, and generates responses in real time. Theo is not a human being; see Section 7. When you opt specific journal entries into "Theo can read," relevant entries may be included as context to personalize Theo's responses.

  • Journals. A private journaling feature where you can record reflections, prayers, and spiritual thoughts. Each journal entry has a privacy level you control: Private (readable only by you), "Theo can read" (opt-in to let Theo reference it in conversation), or Shared to a Group (you select a short excerpt to post to a group's feed).

  • Friends & Groups. Add other users as friends, create or join groups (including churches), and view shared milestones and readings. Group leaderboards show usernames, display names, and streak counts to group members. Churches may enable a public leaderboard at theo.app/c/; your profile appears on it only if you have "discoverable by username" enabled in privacy settings.

  • Voluntary App Blocking. Tools that allow you to voluntarily restrict access to selected applications on your device for a period of time you choose, using Apple's Screen Time and FamilyControls frameworks.

  • Focus Timer Sessions. Timed sessions during which selected apps are blocked, with optional resistance features designed as personal accountability tools.

  • Multi-Day Fasting. An extended app blocking feature that allows you to voluntarily restrict access to selected applications for multiple days.

  • Bible Reader. Access to Bible text from public domain translations, with highlighting, note-taking, and bookmarking capabilities.

  • Reading Plans. Curated multi-day Scripture reading plans on various topics.

  • Gamification. A system of points, streaks, badges, and faith levels designed to encourage consistent engagement.

  • Verse of the Day. A daily Scripture selection with an accompanying prayer prompt.

  • Push Notifications. Optional reminders for sessions, streaks, reading plans, and social events (such as friend requests).

  • Feedback Submission. The ability to send feedback about the App directly to the development team.

5. User Accounts and Your Data

5.1 Account Creation and Sign-In

To access account-dependent features (subscriptions, journals, friends, groups, church leaderboards, cloud sync of streaks and reading progress), you must create an account. You can sign in using:

  • Sign in with Apple — Apple's authentication service.
  • Sign in with Google — Google Identity Services.

These services handle your authentication. We do not directly store your Apple ID or Google credentials.

5.2 Account Data and Cloud Storage

When you create an account, we store the following information on our backend provider, Supabase (a PostgreSQL database hosted in the United States):

  • Email Address. When you sign in with Apple or Google, we receive your email address (which may be your real email or, in the case of Sign in with Apple's Hide My Email feature, a private relay address like xyz@privaterelay.appleid.com). We store this email address in Supabase's auth.users table.
  • Profile: Your chosen username, display name, and optional avatar URL (provided by Apple or Google during sign-in).
  • Subscription Status: Your current subscription plan and renewal date.
  • Push Notification Tokens: For delivering friend requests and reading reminders.
  • Streaks and Reading Progress: Your daily reading streaks, current reading plan progress, and session history.
  • Journals: Your journal entries in their entirety, with privacy levels you control (see Section 5.3). Full journal text is stored in Supabase regardless of privacy level; the privacy level controls who can read it and whether it is processed by AI.
  • Journal Embeddings: Numerical vector representations of journal entries you've opted into "Theo can read," used for semantic search and context retrieval.
  • Friends and Group Memberships: Your friend connections and the groups/churches you've joined.
  • Feed Posts: Excerpts you choose to share to group feeds.
  • Leaderboard Records: Your streak count and rank within groups you've joined, and optionally on public church leaderboards.

All account data is protected by Supabase's row-level security, which ensures you can only read and modify your own data, plus data within groups you belong to (at permissions set by group admins).

5.3 Journal Privacy Levels

Each journal entry has a privacy level only you can change:

  • Private (default). Stored in your account and readable only by you. This data is never shared, never processed by AI, and not visible to other users.

  • Theo can read (opt-in). When you opt a specific entry into this level, the entry's text is sent to OpenAI to generate a numerical embedding (a vector used for semantic search). OpenAI processes it and returns the embedding; per their API data usage policy, the input is retained for abuse monitoring for up to 30 days and is not used to train OpenAI's models. The embedding is stored in your Supabase account so the Theo assistant can retrieve and reference relevant entries in conversation. The full journal text remains stored in Supabase (see Section 5.2). If you later change the entry back to Private or delete it, the embedding is removed within seconds and Theo can no longer reference it.

  • Shared to a Group. You compose a short excerpt and post it to a specific group's feed. Only that group's members can see the excerpt. The full entry remains Private to you, stored in Supabase, and is never shared or processed.

You are solely responsible for the content you choose to share to groups or for opting into "Theo can read." Do not share sensitive personal information, financial details, or content you would not want group members to see.

5.4 Friends, Groups, and Leaderboards

  • Friends. When you add a user as a friend or receive a friend request, their username and display name are visible to you, and your username and display name are visible to them.

  • Groups. When you join or create a group, you can see other members' usernames, display names, optional avatars, and streak counts. Group admins manage membership and can remove members or dissolve the group (except churches, which have admin handoff rules).

  • Church Leaderboards. If your church enables a public leaderboard, a web page at theo.app/c/ displays the top ranking members' usernames, display names, and streak positions. Your profile appears on a public leaderboard only if two conditions are both true: (1) your church has opted into a public leaderboard, and (2) you have enabled "discoverable by username" in your privacy settings. You can opt out of discoverability or leave the church at any time.

5.5 Theo Assistant: AI Providers, and Conversation Memory

Theo is an artificial intelligence system, not a person and not a member of clergy. Every reply it gives is machine-generated. We tell you this in the app before your first message, in accordance with Article 50(1) of the EU Artificial Intelligence Act (Regulation (EU) 2024/1689), applicable since 2 August 2026, and California Senate Bill 243, in force since 1 January 2026.

Two providers are involved, in different roles:

  • Anthropic PBC (United States) generates Theo's replies, using the Claude Haiku 4.5 model.
  • OpenAI, L.L.C. (United States) generates embeddings for journal entries you opt into "Theo can read." OpenAI plays no part in generating Theo's replies.

Through our AWS Lambda proxy we send to Anthropic: (a) your chat message text, (b) your first name, if provided, (c) your denomination, if provided, (d) the conversation turn count, (e) excerpts from journals you opted into "Theo can read," and (f) summarised memory of earlier conversations. We send to OpenAI only the text of journal entries you opted in, and only to create the embedding.

We do not send your email address, surname, payment information, location, Private journal entries, or your blocked-app selections.

Because your denomination and your faith-related content reveal religious belief, we ask for your explicit, separate consent before your first message — a screen naming both providers, describing what is sent, and requiring you to tap "Agree & Continue." Declining leaves every other feature of the App working. You may withdraw consent at any time in Settings → Privacy.

Neither Anthropic nor OpenAI uses your data to train models. Both operate under commercial API terms excluding API traffic from training. Anthropic deletes API inputs and outputs within 30 days; OpenAI retains them up to 30 days for abuse monitoring, then deletes them.

We do not retain the raw text of your chat messages. Our Lambda forwards them and discards them. We do store summarised conversation memories in AWS DynamoDB so Theo has continuity across sessions; these expire automatically after 180 days (60 days for short-term context). If you delete your account, deletion of these memories begins immediately and normally completes within minutes; if a service is briefly unavailable we retry with exponential backoff for up to 24 hours.

We may change AI models or providers to improve quality, reliability, or cost. Where the change is material we will notify you in the App and obtain fresh consent before sending your data to a new provider.
